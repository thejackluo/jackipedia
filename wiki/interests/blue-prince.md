# Blue Prince

**Category:** Interests / Games
**Genre:** Puzzle / Roguelike / Mystery
**Platform:** PC, consoles
**Last updated:** 2026-04-06

## Overview

Blue Prince is a puzzle roguelike game about a mysterious manor with rooms that shift each time you enter. You inherit the estate and try to reach Room 46 - a room that may or may not exist. Every run rearranges the layout. The mystery deepens the further you get.

The core loop: draft rooms from a hand of cards, place them into the manor grid, explore, gather resources, and try to push deeper than the last run. It is a game about information accumulation across failed attempts. Each run teaches you something even if you don't reach your goal.

## What Makes It Interesting

The game operates on two levels simultaneously. On the surface it is a resource management puzzle - you need keys to open doors, drafting the right rooms matters, and positioning affects what connects to what. Underneath that is a genuine mystery: documents, letters, and objects found in rooms slowly reveal what happened in the manor and why Room 46 matters.

The roguelike structure means the mystery unfolds non-linearly. You might learn the ending before you understand the beginning. Pieces arrive out of order and you assemble them run by run.

This structure - information arriving fragmented, meaning emerging slowly through repeated attempts - is unusual in games. Most games give you story in sequence. Blue Prince gives it to you the way memory actually works.

## The Aesthetic

The manor is rendered in a muted, slightly dreamlike palette. Stone corridors, candlelit rooms, old furniture. The atmosphere is quiet and slightly off - the kind of place where something happened a long time ago and the building still remembers.

For someone drawn to liminal spaces, loop structures, and the idea that reality has hidden layers (see [[writings/limbinal]], [[interests/steins-gate]]), Blue Prince is hitting specific notes.
